Buying a rental in an LLC: what lenders actually need

Jayson Cain, Mortgage Loan Originator, NMLS #2270200 Updated October 4, 2026· 5 min read

Short answer

Conventional loans are generally made to individuals, so if you want to close in an LLC, you're usually looking at a DSCR or other business-purpose loan. Expect to provide your formation documents, operating agreement and EIN, and to sign a personal guarantee.

Which loans allow an LLC?

ConventionalDSCR / business-purpose
Close in an LLCGenerally no; title in your nameCommonly allowed
Who signsYou, as an individualThe LLC, usually with a personal guarantee from the owners
Qualifies onYour personal incomeThe property's rent

Documents lenders usually ask for

  • Articles of organization filed with the state
  • Operating agreement showing every member and their ownership share
  • EIN letter from the IRS
  • Certificate of good standing from the state
  • Personal ID and credit for each guarantor

Moving a property into an LLC after closing

Some investors buy in their own name with a conventional loan and transfer title to an LLC later. Most mortgages include a due-on-sale clause, and a transfer can give the lender the right to call the loan. Talk to your lender and a real estate attorney before you record a new deed.

California costs to know

California LLCs generally owe an annual tax to the Franchise Tax Board, even in a year with no income. Check the current amount and filing rules with the FTB, and ask your CPA whether an LLC makes sense for you.

Quick answers

Can I get a conventional loan in an LLC?

Generally no. Conventional loans sold to Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac are made to individuals.

Do I still have to personally guarantee an LLC loan?

Usually yes. Most DSCR lenders require the LLC's owners to sign a personal guarantee.

Does an LLC protect me from the mortgage?

Not if you sign a personal guarantee. Ask an attorney about liability protection for your situation.
